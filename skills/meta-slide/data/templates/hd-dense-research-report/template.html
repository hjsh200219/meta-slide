CITY CYCLING REPORT 2025 · TRENDSEXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Six findings from five years of trip data

01

Trips are up 2.3 times

Annual trips rose from 52 million to 121 million between 2020 and 2025.

02

Commuting leads growth

Commuting reached 46% of trips and supplied 58% of the added volume.

03

Lanes follow demand

612 km of protected lanes were added, mostly on existing commuter corridors.

04

Safety improved

The rider injury rate fell 19% even as exposure more than doubled.

05

Growth is uneven

The newest district grew three times faster than the old town.

06

Summer still peaks

May to October carry 66% of annual trips, with a clear August high.

Source: city transport monitoring centre; anonymised bike-share orders; author estimates.3

Commuting grew from one third to nearly half of all trips

Fig. 5 Trip purpose, 2020 and 2025 (% of trips)

Reading the chart

Commuting rose 12 points, from 34% to 46%.

Daily-life trips such as shopping and school runs fell 7 points as a share, but still grew in absolute numbers.

Leisure and fitness lost 5 points of share while holding steady at about 24 million trips.

Source: city transport monitoring centre; trip-purpose survey, n = 18,400.5

CITY CYCLING REPORT 2025 · TRENDSCONCLUSIONS

What the data asks of the next five years

A

Keep building where people already ride

Lanes on commuter corridors produced the largest gains per kilometre.

B

Close the gap in slower districts

North Star and West Suburb grew at less than half the rate of the newest area.

C

Protect the safety record

Injury rates fell only where separation from traffic was added.

D

Measure the same way every year

Keep the same sources and definitions so the trend stays comparable.
